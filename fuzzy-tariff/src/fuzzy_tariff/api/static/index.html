1 Problem and Proposed System

Under the Swiss Federal Electricity Act (StromVG revision, Jan 2026), smart meters roll out mandatory dynamic retail tariffs consisting of two components: (1) a 15-minute wholesale spot energy price (ENTSO-E), and (2) a non-linear grid-use tariff scaling with transformer net load squared ($load^2$).

The Problem: Household end-users face high price volatility and lack time or expertise to interpret raw market equations. Standard fixed-threshold algorithms fail because prices fluctuate between seasons (e.g. €40/MWh is a summer peak but a winter bargain), leading to severe "label flickering" and excessive on/off switching. Furthermore, multiple heavy loads (EV, heat pump, dryer) turning on at once risks tripping home fuses (16A / 25A) and causing secondary grid congestion peaks.

Proposed System: A hierarchical Two-Stage Mamdani Fuzzy Inference System coupled with a Constraint-Driven Concurrency Scheduler. Stage 1 evaluates price percentiles and grid strain into a continuous suitability score $S \in [0, 100]$. Stage 2 models household urgency. The smart scheduler sequences heavy loads under home breaker limits without requiring manual market inputs from the user.

2 Data Collection

The system operates on real 2025 Swiss electricity market data sampled at 15-minute intervals (35,040 intervals per year, 96 intervals per day):

1. Wholesale Spot Price (ENTSO-E): Day-ahead auction prices in EUR/MWh converted to CHF Rappen/kWh.
2. Swissgrid Substation Net Load: Transformer active power in MW measuring grid loading.
3. Ancillary Services (SDL): Swissgrid reserve power levy (~0.27 Rp./kWh).
4. Total Retail Tariff: T(t) = Energy(t) + GridTariff(net_load(t)²) + SDL
3 Cleaning and Normalising

Raw data is prepared through robust statistical normalization across each 24-hour cycle:

1. Price Percentile P ∈ [0, 100]: P(t) = 100 × (TotalTariff(t) - MinDaily) / (MaxDaily - MinDaily)
2. Absolute Price A ∈ [0, 1]: A(t) = TotalTariff(t) in CHF/kWh (ensures global cost awareness).
3. Grid Congestion G ∈ [0, 1]: G(t) = max(0, min(1, (NetLoad(t) - 100) / 100))
4. Price Trend T ∈ [-1, 1]: T(t) = sign(ΔP) × min(1, |ΔP| / 10 Rp./kWh)
4 Membership Functions and Fuzzification

Continuous inputs are mapped to fuzzy linguistic terms using triangular and trapezoidal membership functions:

Price Percentile P: Cheap [0, 0, 20, 40], Average [25, 45, 60], Expensive [45, 70, 100, 100]
Grid Congestion G: Low [0, 0, 0.2, 0.4], Medium [0.25, 0.5, 0.7], High [0.55, 0.8, 1.0, 1.0]
Stage 1 Suitability Output S: Avoid [0, 0, 15, 30], Wait [20, 35, 50], Okay [40, 52, 65], Good [58, 72, 85], Ideal [75, 88, 100, 100]
5 Rulebase and Defuzzification

Stage 1 uses 19 Mamdani Linguistic Rules defined by power engineering heuristics. When price is cheap and the grid is empty, suitability is Ideal. If the grid is overloaded, high grid tariffs override cheap spot prices to prevent blackouts.

Defuzzification: Aggregated fuzzy output is defuzzified to a crisp score $S \in [0, 100]$ using the Centroid (Center of Gravity) method:

Center of Gravity Formula:
S = ∫ y · μ_B(y) dy / ∫ μ_B(y) dy
6 System Architecture and Output (Tentative)

The live application running on this server is the exact production realization of this architecture:

1. Data Ingestion: 96 15-min intervals ingested automatically from Swiss dynamic curves.
2. Stage 1 Mamdani FIS: Calculates hourly Suitability Score S(t) ∈ [0, 100].
3. Stage 2 Appliance Advisor: Fuzzifies Urgency U = (Duration / Deadline) to decide Start Now vs. Wait.
4. Concurrency Limit Scheduler: Staggers heavy loads into the best S(t) slots without exceeding the user's breaker concurrency limit.
